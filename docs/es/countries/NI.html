FSP member parties together: 75 of 90 (83%) — a majority on their own. The FSP bloc holds a majority in this chamber in its own right.

Government: Daniel Ortega (FSLN) — commanded a working majority. FSP party in government: yes. Reported result of an election the OAS, EU and US said was neither free nor fair: authorities had jailed or forced into exile the leading opposition candidates (seven presidential aspirants disqualified or arrested) and the main opposition coalitions were barred or boycotted. The minor parties that ran alongside the FSLN were characterised as collaborationist by the opposition and independent press. The 90 elected seats shown exclude the two ex-officio seats (outgoing president and presidential runner-up); the FSLN 75 / PLC 10 / others 5 split follows the Wikipedia and Election Guide tallies, while the cited IPU Parline source records the PLC at 9 — sources conflict on the minor-party seats and the split shown is not adjudicated here. ALBA-TCP, by contrast, welcomed the result. (fuente 1, fuente 2, fuente 3)

FSP member parties together: 63 of 92 (68%) — a majority on their own. The FSP bloc holds a majority in this chamber in its own right.

Government: Daniel Ortega (FSLN) — commanded a working majority. FSP party in government: yes. The FSLN jumped to 63 of 92 seats (90 elected plus two ex-officio) — its first supermajority, above the two-thirds needed to amend the constitution, later used for the 2014 reform abolishing presidential term limits. The election itself was contested: the constitution then barred consecutive re-election, and Ortega's candidacy rested on a 2009 ruling by the FSLN-aligned Supreme Court's constitutional chamber setting the ban aside. Runner-up Fabio Gadea (PLI) rejected the results as fraudulent, and EU and OAS observer missions reported serious irregularities and a lack of transparency; the government and its allies maintained the vote was valid. Cambios (era FSP)
The judiciary has been consolidated under FSLN control. In 2009 the CSJ Constitutional Chamber set aside the constitutional ban on consecutive presidential re-election, enabling Ortega’s continued rule; later codified by constitutional reform (2014).
Cuánto permanece
The court remains under FSLN control; after the 2009 ruling and 2014 constitutional reform there is no independent bloc, and the judiciary has been central to Ortega and Murillo's consolidation of power. Exact 2026 roster not individually itemised here.
