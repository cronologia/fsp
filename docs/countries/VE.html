FSP member parties together: 98 of 165 (59%) — a majority on their own. The FSP bloc holds a majority in this chamber in its own right.

Government: Hugo Chávez (PSUV) — commanded a working majority. FSP party in government: yes. The last National Assembly election before 2015 whose results were broadly recognized at home and abroad. The PSUV kept a clear majority (98 of 165) but lost the two-thirds it had enjoyed after the opposition's 2005 boycott. The opposition MUD argued the outcome was engineered: the 2009 electoral law (LOPE) had replaced proportional allocation with mostly winner-take-all districts and redrawn boundaries, so the PSUV took ~59% of seats on roughly 48% of the vote while the MUD's ~47% yielded 65 seats; the government maintained the rules applied equally to all. PPT ran separately from both blocs. (source 1, source 2, source 3)

High court — Tribunal Supremo de Justicia (TSJ)

Seats
32
Appointment
Elected by the National Assembly.
Changes (FSP era)
Widely documented capture: the 2004 Organic Law expanded the TSJ from 20 to 32 magistrates, giving the government a majority; in Dec 2015 the outgoing pro-government Assembly appointed 13 magistrates before the opposition majority was seated. The TSJ subsequently annulled or curtailed acts of the opposition-led National Assembly (2016–2017).
How much remains
The court has remained PSUV-aligned ever since the 2004 expansion and 2015 packing; a 2022 reform cut it to 20 magistrates, re-appointed by the chavista-controlled National Assembly. No independent/opposition-appointed bloc remains.
