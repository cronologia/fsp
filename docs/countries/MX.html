High court — Suprema Corte de Justicia de la Nación (SCJN)

Seats
9
Appointment
As of the 2024 reform, justices are ELECTED by popular vote (previously: nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate).
Changes (FSP era)
Landmark change: the 2024 judicial reform (promulgated Sept 2024 under AMLO, championed by Sheinbaum) makes ALL judges — including the Supreme Court — elected by popular vote, cuts the SCJN from 11 to 9 justices, and shortens tenure from 15 to 12 years. The first judicial elections were held 1 June 2025 and a newly elected court was seated in Sept 2025. Critics (Mexican Bar, HRW, scholars) warn it undermines judicial independence; supporters say it democratises the judiciary.
How much remains
The pre-reform court was effectively replaced. An entirely new 9-justice court was elected on 1 June 2025 and seated 1 September 2025 (12-year terms), with Hugo Aguilar Ortiz as chief justice (2025–2027). Three former AMLO-appointed justices (Lenia Batres, Yasmín Esquivel, Loretta Ortiz) were re-elected. Turnout was about 13%, and the new court is widely assessed as aligned with the ruling Morena party.
