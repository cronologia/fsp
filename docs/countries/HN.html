FSP member parties (highlighted FSP): Libertad y Refundación (LIBRE). LIBRE, formed in 2011 by supporters of the ousted Manuel Zelaya, is Honduras's FSP member. Before 2011 there was no FSP party in Congress.

FSP member parties together: 35 of 128 (27%) — 30 seats short of the 65-seat majority. The FSP parties sat in opposition.

Government: Nasry Asfura (PN) — lacked a majority. FSP party in government: no. The Partido Nacional's Nasry Asfura won the presidency and PN took a plurality of Congress (49 of 128); no party reached the 65-seat majority, so lawmaking depends on cross-party alliances. LIBRE, the FSP member, returned to opposition with 35 seats. The CNE issued its final declaration on 30 December 2025, having declared Asfura the presidential winner on 24 December with ~40.2% of the vote; both LIBRE and the Liberal Party's Salvador Nasralla disputed the count. PL and LIBRE are counted as opposition to the PN government but are ideological opposites and do not form a common bloc; PINU-SD (2) and the Christian Democrats (1) hold the balance. Government: Xiomara Castro (LIBRE) — lacked a majority. FSP party in government: yes. LIBRE won a plurality (50/128); even with its electoral ally PSH (10) the government bloc held 60 seats — 5 short of a majority. The gap showed immediately: in January 2022 a group of LIBRE dissidents backed a rival congressional leadership, splitting the chamber before a negotiated settlement. Castro governed without reliable control of Congress. (source 1, source 2)

2016–2023 independent 0 of 15 The 2016–2023 Supreme Court was elected in 2016 by a Congress controlled by the Hernández (Partido Nacional, non-FSP) government; LIBRE had no role in its selection. The 2012 removal of four Constitutional Chamber justices (the 'technical coup') and the 2015 ruling that lifted the presidential re-election ban both came from courts seated under non-FSP governments — the FSP party never controlled these benches. (source 1, source 2)
2023–present partial 6 of 15 In February 2023 the LIBRE-led Congress elected the 2023–2030 Supreme Court (the 15 magistrates require a two-thirds vote, from a list vetted by a nominating board). After three weeks of deadlock the seats were distributed by a negotiated tripartite pact — about 6 of the 15 magistrates were counted as LIBRE-aligned, 5 as Partido Nacional and 4 as Partido Liberal. It was LIBRE's largest-ever share of the bench but short of a majority; a PSH deputy criticised it as a new tripartite 'reparto'. (source 1, source 2, source 3)
