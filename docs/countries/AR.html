High court — Corte Suprema de Justicia de la Nación

Seats
5
Appointment
Nominated by the President, confirmed by 2/3 of the Senate.
Changes (FSP era)
In 1990 President Menem (Peronist, pre-Kirchner) expanded the court from 5 to 9 (Law 23.774), creating an "automatic majority". In 2006 the Kirchnerist government reduced it back to 5 (Law 26.183) and had tightened the appointment process (Decree 222/03, 2003). So the packing predates the Kirchnerist (FSP-affiliated) governments, which actually de-packed it — a useful counter to a simplistic 'FSP packs courts' reading.
How much remains
After the retirements of Highton (2021) and Maqueda (2024), the court fell below its 5-seat complement to 3 justices; President Milei's attempts to fill the seats by decree (Lijo, García-Mansilla) failed in the Senate (2025), leaving a deadlock as of 2026.

1863 founding The Corte Suprema was installed under the 1853 Constitution with 5 justices. 5 Mitre (constitutional founding)
1947 purge Perón's Congress impeached and removed 4 of the 5 justices (Sagarna, Repetto, Nazar Anchorena, Ramos Mejía), replacing them with Peronist-aligned appointees — the start of a long pattern of incoming governments reshaping the court. 5 Juan Domingo Perón (PJ)
1958 reform Law 15.271 expanded the court from 5 to 7 members. 7 Frondizi (UCRI)
1966 reform Law 16.895 returned the court to 5 members. 5 Onganía (military 'Revolución Argentina')
1976–1983 composition Last military dictatorship: the junta replaced the court, which operated under the de facto regime. ? 5 Military junta
1990 packing Law 23.774 (Menem) expanded the court from 5 to 9, creating the 'automatic majority'. 9 Carlos Menem (PJ)
2003 reform Decree 222/03 (Néstor Kirchner) opened Supreme Court nominations to public scrutiny, applied to the post-Menem vacancies. 9 Néstor Kirchner (FpV)
2006 reform Law 26.183 (promoted by Cristina Fernández de Kirchner) reduced the court from 9 back to 5, reversing Menem's expansion. 5 Kirchnerist government (FpV)
