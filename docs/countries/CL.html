FSP member parties (highlighted FSP): Partido Socialista de Chile (PS), Partido Comunista de Chile (PCCh). Chile's FSP members are the Partido Socialista (PS) and the Partido Comunista (PCCh); the Forum's own roster also lists smaller groups (Izquierda Ciudadana, Partido Humanista, MIR). Crucially, these parties have always governed as a minority faction inside broad center-left coalitions — the Concertación (1990–2010, led by the non-FSP Christian Democrats), the Nueva Mayoría (which added the PCCh from 2013), and Boric's Apruebo Dignidad (2022–, Frente Amplio + PCCh). The PS and PCCh have never commanded a congressional majority on their own: the 2025 seat tables below show them holding just 22 of the Chamber's 155 seats and 10 of the Senate's 50 — a minority even within the Unidad por Chile bloc they share with the (non-FSP) Frente Amplio, PPD, Christian Democrats, Radicals and Liberals. Boric's 2022–2026 government likewise lacked a majority in either chamber.

Government: José Antonio Kast (Partido Republicano) — lacked a majority. FSP party in government: no. The Chamber was fully renewed on 16 November 2025 for 2026–2030. President-elect Kast's Partido Republicano anchors the Cambio por Chile bloc; with the traditional right (Chile Grande y Unido) the governing right holds 76 of 155 — short of the 78-seat majority, so it relies on the Partido de la Gente (14) and independents. The FSP members PS (11) and PCCh (11) sit in opposition inside the 61-seat Unidad por Chile bloc and are a small minority of the chamber; the non-FSP Frente Amplio (17) is the bloc's largest party.
